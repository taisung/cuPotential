# TYK2 alchemical sample Hamiltonian and reciprocal derivatives

Date: 2026-11-18

Source version: 0.3.5, checkout `8e62290997297fb7122920ab5718d314b49264c2`

Updated: 2026-11-18, with corrections after an independent review. Labels: documented (a source states it), calculated (counted in the serialized XML or computed from it), measured (a run for this note, section 5), inferred.

The `ejm_31` to `ejm_42` sample loads one prepared TYK2 hybrid Hamiltonian and evaluates it through stock OpenMM and cuPo. Each force evaluation uses the effective charges for one lambda setting and performs one Coulomb reciprocal solve. The sample checks energies and forces after live parameter updates. It computes neither an energy derivative with respect to lambda nor a free energy. [Sample entry point](../plugins/openmm/samples/alchemical/run#L5) · [Comparison and dynamics](../plugins/openmm/samples/alchemical/sample.py#L230)

## 1. The endpoint parameters in the hybrid

OpenFE's `HybridTopologyFactory` takes two parameterized endpoint systems and a ligand atom map. The 34,082-particle hybrid shares the environment and 31 mapped ligand atoms. It contains one old-only hydrogen and four new-only atoms (calculated from the XML). Both unique regions have coordinates throughout the transformation (inferred from the hybrid design). The factory writes their coupling rules and the mapped atoms' endpoint parameters into one OpenMM `System`. `sample.py` deserializes that same XML twice and initializes both contexts from the bundled frame. [Fixture provenance](../plugins/openmm/samples/alchemical/data/README.md#L15) · [Pinned factory](https://github.com/OpenFreeEnergy/openfe/blob/1d4610ba845f6fd7b9a107b262a345d1a063d89a/src/openfe/protocols/openmm_rfe/_rfe_utils/relative.py#L24) · [System loading](../plugins/openmm/samples/alchemical/sample.py#L207)

**Electrostatics.** The native `NonbondedForce` stores 36 particle charge offsets. With electrostatic globals denoted by $\lambda_q$, the effective charges are

| Region | Effective charge |
| --- | --- |
| Environment | Fixed charge |
| Mapped ligand core, 31 atoms | $q_A+\lambda_{q,\mathrm{core}}(q_B-q_A)$ |
| Old-only, one atom | $(1-\lambda_{q,\mathrm{delete}})q_A$ |
| New-only, four atoms | $\lambda_{q,\mathrm{insert}}q_B$ |

The direct Coulomb term and the reciprocal solver use the same effective charges. Coulomb energy depends on charge products, so the Hamiltonian can vary nonlinearly even where the charges vary linearly. Native exceptions can carry their own charge-product offsets. [Charge construction](https://github.com/OpenFreeEnergy/openfe/blob/1d4610ba845f6fd7b9a107b262a345d1a063d89a/src/openfe/protocols/openmm_rfe/_rfe_utils/relative.py#L1743) · [CUDA offset application](../plugins/openmm/cpp/platforms/cuda/src/nonbonded/CudaNonbondedKernels.cu#L423) · [Shared charge weights](../plugins/openmm/cpp/platforms/cuda/src/nonbonded/CudaNonbondedKernels.cu#L503)

**Lennard-Jones.** The XML custom force first mixes the pair's $\sigma$ arithmetically and $\epsilon$ geometrically within each endpoint. It then interpolates those pair parameters with the core, insert, or delete sterics global. The absent endpoint has zero well depth for a pair involving a unique atom. For such a pair, let $a=1-\lambda_{\mathrm{sterics,insert}}$ on the new side or $a=\lambda_{\mathrm{sterics,delete}}$ on the old side. Gapsys v2 uses ordinary LJ above $r_{\mathrm{LJ}}=0.85\,\sigma[(26/7)a]^{1/6}$ and a quadratic continuation below it. For a core pair with no unique atoms, $a=0$ and the interpolated LJ has the ordinary radial form. [Serialized hybrid](../plugins/openmm/samples/alchemical/data/system.xml.gz) · [Pinned sterics expression](https://github.com/OpenFreeEnergy/openfe/blob/1d4610ba845f6fd7b9a107b262a345d1a063d89a/src/openfe/protocols/openmm_rfe/_rfe_utils/relative.py#L967)

**Bonded terms and exceptions.** The custom bond and angle expressions support interpolation of force constants and equilibrium geometry. In the shipped XML, all 22 custom bonds and 49 custom angles have identical stored A/B values, and the custom torsion force is empty. The hybrid has 286 native exception-offset records and 13 separate lambda-dependent `CustomBondForce_exceptions` bonds whose native interactions were zeroed. Of the 286 records, 143 belong to `lambda_electrostatics_core` and 143 to `lambda_sterics_core`. Only 62 records, all charge-product offsets of `lambda_electrostatics_core`, are nonzero (calculated). Both physical exception paths use raw distances in this fixture. Reciprocal exclusion corrections use periodic minimum-image distances. [Serialized hybrid](../plugins/openmm/samples/alchemical/data/system.xml.gz) · [Fixture conventions](../plugins/openmm/samples/alchemical/data/README.md#L29) · [DSpace exclusion contract](../include/cupo/dspace.h#L59)

The saved window corresponds to master lambda 0.5 in OpenFE's `namd` schedule. The core and bonded globals are 0.5. In this fixture, `lambda_sterics_core`, `lambda_bonds`, `lambda_angles`, and `lambda_torsions` change no energy (calculated). In the custom nonbonded force, every atom outside the unique regions has identical A and B sigma and epsilon, all `lambda_sterics_core` exception offsets are zero, the custom bonds and angles have identical A/B values, and the custom torsion force is empty. Electrostatics delete and insert are 1 and 0, leaving both unique regions uncharged. Sterics delete and insert are 0.25 and 0.75, so eligible pairs on both unique sides retain 75% of their coupled LJ well depth with $a=0.25$. The sample's sterics perturbation uses 0.35 and 0.65, making the coupled well-depth fraction 65% and increasing the softcore radius. ## 2. Pair masks and the fused cutoff expression

`combine_nonbonded()` checks that the stock PME force and the custom periodic cutoff force agree on particle count, cutoff, switching, tail enablement, and the 55,094 exception/exclusion pairs. It reconstructs ordinary LJ from the native parameters, renames the custom expression's intermediates, and converts the eight custom interaction groups into four fixed membership flags. For disjoint regions A and B, the group contributes `A1*B2 + A2*B1`. A same-region group contributes `A1*A2`. For overlapping sets the code subtracts the product of the two orientations to count the pair once within the group. [Group conversion and compatibility checks](../plugins/openmm/samples/alchemical/sample.py#L57) · [Expression assembly](../plugins/openmm/samples/alchemical/sample.py#L109)

The resulting LJ expression is

```text
ordinary_LJ + mask * select(mask, alchemical_LJ, 0)
```

The mask covers core–environment, core–core, and each unique region's pairs with core, environment, and itself. The unique self-groups exist in the XML, but the old-only region has one atom and all six new-only intragroup pairs are explicit exceptions in this fixture. Environment–environment uses ordinary LJ. Old–new has a zero mask and is explicitly excluded. The native particle epsilon is zero for every ligand atom, so ordinary LJ vanishes on regular ligand-containing pairs. The `select` guards inactive softcore arithmetic. Exclusion cancellation is a separate operation. The NonbondedForce guide states that exclusions remove pair contributions "by repeated evaluation" and that the evaluator may still run on an excluded pair. [Serialized groups and exceptions](../plugins/openmm/samples/alchemical/data/system.xml.gz) · [Fused expression](../plugins/openmm/samples/alchemical/sample.py#L155) · [cuPotential exclusion evaluation](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cupotential/nonbonded-force#exclusion-evaluation)

The plugin constructor copies charges, offsets, and native exceptions. `setCutoffInteraction()` installs the full LJ expression in the direct pair traversal, where the shared switch applies from 0.8 to 0.9 nm. This sample integrates one homogeneous tail for the combined expression. The stock reference computes separate native and grouped custom tails, so the energy can shift even when the pair forces agree. The repository's separate TYK2 benchmark converter preserves the source-specific tail policies. [Plugin call](../plugins/openmm/samples/alchemical/sample.py#L173) · [Benchmark converter](../data/tyk2_alchemical.py#L441)

## 3. Reciprocal work at a given lambda

The plugin builds the effective charge vector again only when a watched global changes ([offset update](../plugins/openmm/cpp/platforms/cuda/src/nonbonded/CudaNonbondedKernels.cu#L601)). Each force evaluation uses the current vector and runs one Coulomb lattice solve. Stock OpenMM uses PME. The cuPo constructor selects Prolate, which also uses a reciprocal mesh. The source force uses PME, not LJPME, so the plugin adds only the Coulomb lattice sum ([LJPME condition](../plugins/openmm/cpp/openmmapi/src/NonbondedForce.cpp#L293)). The homogeneous tail covers LJ beyond the cutoff. The plugin rejects a homogeneous tail together with a dispersion lattice sum ([tail check](../plugins/openmm/cpp/openmmapi/src/NonbondedForce.cpp#L428)). The comparison evaluates both contexts at each setting, giving two independent solves per reported row. Only the cuPo context advances in the 200-step NVT segment. [Default Prolate constructor](../plugins/openmm/cpp/openmmapi/src/NonbondedForce.cpp#L197) · [CUDA reciprocal launch](../plugins/openmm/cpp/platforms/cuda/src/nonbonded/CudaNonbondedKernels.cu#L1597) · [Sample loop](../plugins/openmm/samples/alchemical/sample.py#L244)

## 4. Energy derivatives with respect to lambda

The sample requests potential energy and forces. Its changes to the core charge and sterics globals are finite perturbations used to compare live updates. `combine_nonbonded()` rejects a custom source that registers an energy parameter derivative. Differentiation of the pair expression with respect to distance supplies the radial force. [Converter check](../plugins/openmm/samples/alchemical/sample.py#L104) · [State query](../plugins/openmm/samples/alchemical/sample.py#L201) · [Expression compilation](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cupotential/nonbonded-force#expression-compilation)

An analytic charge derivative can use the mesh potential from the force evaluation. At fixed positions and box, the reciprocal energy has the quadratic form $U_{\mathrm{recip}}=\tfrac12\mathbf q^\mathsf{T}K\mathbf q$ for a symmetric reciprocal operator $K$. Writing the potential at each atom as $\phi_{\mathrm{recip},i}=(K\mathbf q)_i$ gives

$$
\frac{\partial U_{\mathrm{recip}}}{\partial\lambda_k}
=\sum_i \phi_{\mathrm{recip},i}\,\frac{\partial q_i}{\partial\lambda_k}.
$$

Here $\partial q_i/\partial\lambda_k$ is the stored charge offset for global $\lambda_k$. A runtime implementation can gather the reciprocal potential at the atoms with nonzero offsets and reduce the products. One mesh solve then serves all charge globals, but only for the reciprocal term (inferred).

The other charge-dependent terms need their own outputs. The DSpace output call returns only a total energy and forces, with no per-atom potential ([DSpace output](../include/cupo/dspace.h#L196)). These terms are not small. For the Ewald splitting of the stock force at the saved window, the self term contributes +4.452 kJ/mol to $\partial U/\partial\lambda_{\mathrm{electrostatics,core}}$. The 62 nonzero exception charge-product offsets contribute +10.675 kJ/mol. The total derivative is +5.217 kJ/mol (section 5). The self and exception values are calculated from the XML and the frame. The split between terms depends on the splitting function.

The background term also applies. Both endpoints are neutral, but the window carries a net charge of −0.0549 e (calculated). With the stock Ewald $\alpha$ of 2.920 nm⁻¹, the background energy is about −2.1×10⁻⁴ kJ/mol, and its derivative is about +2.5×10⁻⁴ kJ/mol per unit of the core global (calculated). A full derivative must also include direct Coulomb, exclusion corrections, physical exceptions, and any lambda-dependent LJ, tail, or bonded terms.

The public KSpace interface exposes reciprocal energy and coordinate forces. It keeps the mesh potential internal and provides no scalar-potential gather or lambda derivative output ([KSpace convolution and interpolation API](../include/cupo/kspace.h#L252)). The lower-level convolution call can return its real-space result grid ([convolution output](../include/cupo/convolution.h#L182)), and the spreading header provides the stencil weights ([spreading weights](../include/cupo/math/spreading.h#L20)). An engine that does its own spreading and gathering can therefore form $\phi_{\mathrm{recip}}$ from public functions (inferred). The NonbondedForce guide states: "Energy derivatives with respect to global parameters are not provided." ([guide](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cupotential/nonbonded-force)). [Huang et al. (2016)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5713900/) apply PME to continuous constant-pH MD, which needs energy derivatives with respect to titration coordinates. This note did not check the derivation in that paper.

A finite difference of total energy uses $[U(\lambda_k+h)-U(\lambda_k-h)]/(2h)$ at fixed coordinates, box, and other globals. For a charge-changing global, those two energies require different charge vectors and separate reciprocal solves. For a sterics-only global in this fixture, the Coulomb reciprocal contribution is constant across the two evaluations. Each evaluation still runs the solve. The sample combines the source force groups into one group ([group merge](../plugins/openmm/samples/alchemical/sample.py#L27)), so an evaluation restricted to a force group cannot skip the Coulomb terms (inferred). A derivative with respect to master lambda combines the partial derivatives with the component schedule slopes, $dU/d\lambda=\sum_k(\partial U/\partial\lambda_k)(d\lambda_k/d\lambda)$. At the master-lambda 0.5 break in the `namd` charge schedule, the old-charge and new-charge ramps have different one-sided slopes. [Pinned lambda schedule](https://github.com/OpenFreeEnergy/openfe/blob/1d4610ba845f6fd7b9a107b262a345d1a063d89a/src/openfe/protocols/openmm_rfe/_rfe_utils/lambdaprotocol.py#L116)

**Re-evaluation for MBAR and BAR.** MBAR needs the reduced potential $u_l(x_n)=\beta U_l(x_n)$ of each saved configuration $x_n$ at every state $l$. BAR needs it only at the sampled state and its two neighbors. The fixture has no barostat, so no $pV$ term enters (documented). These estimators need energies, not lambda derivatives, so the missing parameter derivatives do not block them. A re-evaluation sets the globals of state $l$ with `setParameter()` and queries the energy at fixed coordinates and box. The sample's window loop does this at one frame, and it checks that the plugin and stock energy changes agree within 0.05 kJ/mol. Each state costs one evaluation of the fused force, with its own reciprocal solve, because the Coulomb and LJ pair terms share that force (inferred). Per saved configuration, MBAR with $K$ states needs $K$ evaluations, BAR needs at most three, and central-difference TI needs two per active global. This note did not check whether an energy-only query skips the force work. ## 5. Checks on the bundled frame

- **Version.** All checks in this section used stock OpenMM 8.1.1 on CUDA. OpenMM 8.4 serialized the fixture, and the plugin package needs OpenMM 8.4 ([0010](0010_nvidia_documentation_review.md)). The checks were not repeated with OpenMM 8.4. The check scripts are not in the repository.
- **Stock sample windows.** OpenMM 8.1.1 CUDA in mixed precision gave energy changes of +0.5215 kJ/mol for the core-charge perturbation and +1.3928 kJ/mol for the sterics perturbation, relative to the prepared window. Restoring the globals reproduced the prepared energy to the printed precision.
- **LJ fusion.** The exact expression emitted by `combine_nonbonded()` was evaluated in a stock CUDA `CustomNonbondedForce`, while native PME and the physical exceptions stayed active. Native regular LJ was disabled in this second system to avoid counting it twice. Across the four settings in mixed precision, the relative force difference was about $4.9\times10^{-7}$ overall and at most $3.4\times10^{-8}$ on the 36 ligand atoms. At the prepared window in double precision, the fused-minus-stock energy difference was −0.000594 kJ/mol with both tails disabled and −0.000999 kJ/mol with the respective sample and stock tail policies enabled. The difference between those gaps isolates the tail-policy shift on that frame.
- **Lambda derivatives.** Stock CUDA returned an empty parameter-derivative map for the fixture. Central differences of the total energy in double precision at the saved frame gave the values below, varying one global at a time (measured).

| Global | $h=10^{-1}$ | $h=10^{-3}$ | $h=10^{-4}$ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `lambda_electrostatics_core` | **+5.2171** | +5.2041 | +5.2005 |
| `lambda_sterics_insert` | not run | −12.1441 | −12.1463 |
| `lambda_sterics_delete` | not run | +2.1872 | +2.1871 |

The derivative units are kJ/mol per unit of the named global. The correct core value is +5.217 kJ/mol. At fixed coordinates, the energy is exactly quadratic in `lambda_electrostatics_core`, because the charges and the exception charge products change linearly with it (calculated). A central difference of a quadratic has no truncation error, so a large step gives the exact slope. A rerun gave +5.21712 at $h=0.2$ and +5.21710 at $h=0.1$ (measured). The small steps are limited by roundoff. The stock double-precision energy is about −4.386×10⁵ kJ/mol. At 41 core values 10⁻⁵ apart, it scatters about the quadratic fit with an rms of 2.7×10⁻⁵ kJ/mol and a maximum of 8.2×10⁻⁵ kJ/mol (measured). A scatter $\delta$ gives a central-difference error up to about $\delta/h$. The $h=10^{-3}$ and $h=10^{-4}$ core values are 0.25% and 0.32% low. The sterics energies are not quadratic, and their two columns agree within the roundoff bound of $h=10^{-3}$.
